# stuck-conditions: constraints, rejected alternatives and rationale

## Rationale
- **Rationale: A failed worker spawn is a condition on its instance.**
  A sudoers line belongs to one host and one role's `igor serve`, so the condition is keyed by both. A key scoped to an item counts each item's failures apart, so no condition opens and every item fails in turn. One scoped to the role would also stop a developer's `igor run`, which has no worker user, and would let another host's clean runs and passing checks end the broken host's count and clear its condition. One scoped to the whole Igor clears only when an App token is minted again, which fixing a sudoers line does not do.
  Evidence: `scopeOf` gives a scope only to `role`, `seat` and `app` keys, and a key of any other shape stops nothing, in `src/conditions.ts` on `main`, so the instance scope is one task 0.3 of the `worker-user` change, #175, adds, https://github.com/adamstallard/igor/blob/b4f8ac0/src/conditions.ts#L66-L77
  Evidence: an Igor-scoped condition has no cooldown and no probe and stays open until `serve` mints a token again, in `src/conditions.ts` on `main`, https://github.com/adamstallard/igor/blob/b4f8ac0/src/conditions.ts#L225-L228
- **Rationale: A condition's scope is its cure's scope.**
  One cure can be met by many Igors, so keying by the process that met it would count one cure once per Igor, back off and clear once per Igor, and leave each Igor to rediscover what the others already recorded. Scoping by cure also keeps the stop as small as it honestly can be: a role refused one command says nothing about any other role. No condition state is stored apart from the log, because a summary beside a log can disagree with it and neither side can then show which is right, while appends from several Igors cannot overwrite each other. A key naming a seat's credential names the seat and not the credential, so one seat's two tokens share a key. One key can also open, clear and reopen several conditions over its life. Without a discriminator, a condition rebuilt from that key cannot say whether it is about the credential resolving now.
  Evidence: a seat's counting loop that already needed the token's fingerprint beside the cure key to tell two credentials apart, #65
  Evidence: a cure key opening, clearing and reopening, so that keying on the cure alone answered for a closed occurrence while a fleet sat stopped, #77
- **Rationale: A condition clears by not recurring, and a probe is what observes that.**
  The cure is usually a configuration change made on a surface with no channel back to the loop, so nobody announces it, and a stopped scope runs nothing and so can never observe that the condition stopped recurring. One probe item buys that observation, and the cooldown bounds the cost of being wrong. The probe is the next item the scope would take, not the one that met the condition, because the condition is about the cure and that item may be unworkable for its own reasons. A changed discriminator is the cure observed directly, so it clears at once rather than keeping an operator who has already fixed the thing waiting out a lengthening backoff.
  Evidence: an item parked since a worker that was not logged in exited 1, a day after its cure landed, released only by a person commenting, #22
  Evidence: a handed-back item suppressed although nothing about the item caused the handoff, #43
